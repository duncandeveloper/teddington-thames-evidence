Evidence · Ownership & decision-making

Who owns Thames Water — and who actually decides

The company that operates Mogden sewage works and proposes to abstract from this reach is owned through a six-company chain, is in the middle of a contested restructuring in which its registered shareholders stand to lose everything, and its flagship local scheme will be decided by a single Secretary of State — not by any vote of Parliament or any council. This page traces ownership to the company register, names the board from its filings, and sets out who can actually influence the decision, and when.

The registered owners — what the company register says

Every entity below is traceable at Companies House, and every link in the chain is a “person with significant control” registration of 75% or more of shares and voting rights. This is the ownership of record — and, as the next section explains, it is no longer the whole story.

Above the chain sits a consortium of investment and pension funds whose reported shareholdings in the Kemble parent have included the Ontario Municipal Employees Retirement System (~32%), the Universities Superannuation Scheme (~20%), Abu Dhabi Investment Authority, British Columbia Investment Management, Hermes, China Investment Corporation, Queensland Investment Corporation, Aquila and the Dutch pension fund PFZW. Kemble Water Holdings itself has a registered statement that no single registrable person controls it — no one investor crosses the 25% control threshold with the register.

These are reported holdings in the parent, stated here for the record. Under the restructuring proposal described below, the consortium’s own statement is that existing shareholders “lose their investment entirely”.

Who actually controls it — a contested question, honestly stated

In 2026 the register and reality have come apart. The facts below are dated deliberately: this section will change, and a reader should know exactly when each statement was true.

  • The debt exceeds the equity’s worth. A creditor group trading as London & Valley Water — around 100 financial institutions holding more than £17bn of senior debt — has proposed a rescue in which it injects £10bn of new capital, writes off £9.6bn of debt, and takes ownership; the registered shareholders lose everything. (Their statement, 21 July 2026.)
  • Government has already rejected one version. In June 2026 the then Environment Secretary declined the earlier proposal as not doing enough for consumers and the environment. The July version adds a ministerial “golden share”, an independent monitor with boardroom access, local-authority representation in a new supervisory structure, a ten-year dividend bar and an expanded social tariff. (As reported and per the consortium’s statement; no decision had been announced when this page was last verified.)
  • Special administration remains the alternative. If no restructuring is approved, the government can apply to place the company into a Special Administration Regime. As at 31 August 2026 that has not happened, and this page does not predict it.
  • The regulator holds its own levers. Ofwat’s £122.7m penalty package (May 2025) is being paid under an agreed plan, and licence conditions constrain dividends and require board independence. Regulatory control operates whoever owns the equity.

The practical point for this reach: whoever ends up owning Thames Water inherits the TDRA application, the Mogden assets and the statutory obligations unchanged. The scheme’s fate does not turn on the ownership fight — it turns on the planning process in the next section.

What they plan for this reach — and how the scheme has already changed

The Teddington Direct River Abstraction scheme would take water from the tidal Thames just below the weir and replace it with treated effluent from a new tertiary treatment plant at Mogden. It reached this point through the statutory water-resources planning route — the regional plan and the company’s Water Resources Management Plan — and was directed into the nationally-significant regime by a section 35 Direction in December 2023.

  • Statutory consultation, 2025 — the main public consultation on the scheme design.
  • Supplementary consultation, 21 May – 18 June 2026 — on design changes near Burnell Avenue and Beaufort Road.
  • Changes made (per Thames Water): above-ground works at Ham Playing Fields removed; the outfall position revised; the tertiary treatment plant relocated within the Mogden works. Anyone objecting in 2027 needs to address the scheme as it now is, not as first consulted.
  • DCO application: expected early 2027, delayed from 2026 “to allow for further feedback” (Thames Water project page, read 31 August 2026).

Who decides — and what each actor can actually do

TDRA is a Nationally Significant Infrastructure Project (Planning Inspectorate reference WA010006). That puts it under the Planning Act 2008: an appointed Examining Authority examines, and the Secretary of State decides. There is no parliamentary vote, and no local vote.

The window that matters

After the application is accepted, the Planning Inspectorate opens a Relevant Representation window — the period in which any person, group or council registers as an Interested Party. Registration is what confers the right to submit evidence and be heard in the examination. On the current timetable that window is likely to fall in 2027; it lasts a matter of weeks and closes on a fixed date published on the project page. Evidence that is not registered through that window has no formal route into the examination, however good it is. This page will carry the exact dates when they are published; the authoritative source is the Planning Inspectorate project page for WA010006.

How to read this

Caveats a regulator would press — stated up front

  • Ownership facts here decay fast. Every statement is dated, and the page carries a “last verified” date. If the restructuring completes or special administration begins, the control section is out of date from that day and will be revised.
  • Naming a director states a public fact, nothing more. Appointments are from the statutory register. Nothing on this page suggests any named individual has acted improperly.
  • A rejected proposal is a decision about a proposal. It is not a finding against the company or its creditors, and this page does not treat it as one.
  • Foreign and state-linked investors are reported plainly. Their presence on the register is a fact; no inference is invited beyond it.
  • “Nationalisation is likely” is commentary, and it is attributed or omitted here. As at the last-verified date, no special administration order existed.
  • The scheme description is the applicant’s. What TDRA would do is stated from Thames Water’s own project material; this programme’s assessment of its effects belongs on the evidence pages, not here.
