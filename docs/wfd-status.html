Evidence · Water Framework Directive status

Neither Thames water body on this reach reaches "good"

Under the Water Framework Directive, the freshwater river above Teddington is classed Poor and the tidal body below it Moderate — both failing chemical status — and both have missed "good" for three assessment cycles. This is the Environment Agency's own classification, its named reasons, and the objective it has pushed out to 2063.

The headline figures

The reach spans two WFD water bodies: a heavily-modified river from Egham down to Teddington Weir, and the transitional (tidal) "Thames Middle" body below it. Neither is at "good", both fail on chemistry, and on both the Agency names sewage discharge among the reasons why.

Why it isn't "good": the EA's named reasons

For Cycle 3 the Agency publishes Reasons for Not Achieving Good (RNAGs) — each failing element tied to a business sector and activity. On both water bodies, "Waste water treatment — Sewage discharge (continuous)" is a named pressure (highlighted rows below), alongside agriculture, urban runoff and legacy chemicals.

What the tidal "Moderate" is — and isn't

The tidal ecological Moderate is not a picture of dead biology. Under the estuarine classification tools, the living elements score well; the Moderate is driven by plants (angiosperms / saltmarsh), a supporting nutrient (nitrogen) and a specific pollutant (zinc).

The "good" objective, relaxed to 2063

WFD lets the Agency set alternative objectives — a later deadline, or a lower target than "good" — where meeting good by the default date is judged technically infeasible or disproportionately costly. On this reach the chemical "good" objective has been pushed to 2063 on both bodies, and the ecological/overall objectives are set no higher than the current class. The reason codes matter for the accountability question, because they include "disproportionately expensive — disproportionate burdens".

That is the documentary backdrop to the fines-versus-investment debate — but it is a statement about cost/feasibility judgements, not proof of any single party's culpability. RAW-PULL

How to read this

Caveats a regulator would press — stated up front

  • The chemical FAIL is largely a national artefact. The synchronised 2015 → 2019 Good→Fail flip on both bodies is driven by ubiquitous persistent, bioaccumulative toxicants — mercury-in-biota, PBDE and PFOS — newly added to the chemical standards in Cycle 3. These fail in almost every English water body, carry a documented "natural recovery time", and are not solely attributable to Thames Water. Do not read the chemical Fail as a local, present-day collapse. PUBLISHED context on the standards; RAW-PULL for the classification.
  • Tidal biology is healthy. Under the estuarine tools, Fish, Phytoplankton, Invertebrates and Macroalgae all score Good/High on Thames Middle. The Moderate is chemistry (zinc), a plant/nutrient signal (angiosperms, saltmarsh, nitrogen) and hydromorphology — not dead biology. On the river, Invertebrates even improved Poor → High by the 2022 interim, though phytobenthos (the phosphate-sensitive element) held the ecological class at Poor. RAW-PULL
  • Named pressure ≠ sole cause; correlation ≠ causation. A RNAG names sewage discharge as a pressure, but the same elements list agriculture, urban drainage and legacy contamination too. The classification identifies pressures; it does not apportion blame or prove that any one discharge caused any one failure. RAW-PULL
  • Zinc — the tidal Moderate driver — is unmonitored in open data. Zinc has zero records at all 12 public water-quality points on the reach, so the Agency's own zinc classification cannot be corroborated from the open archive. A named cause we cannot independently check. PROVEN GAP
  • Water-body codes on this reach do not line up. The tidal classification here is GB530603911402 (Thames Middle), but Mogden's sewage outfall and the tidal fish/benthic monitoring are coded GB530603911403 — an adjacent body on the same reach. The sewage RNAGs cited above belong to …402; reconcile the codes before attributing a specific outfall to a specific classification. RAW-PULL
  • Attribution and interims are Cycle-3-only / partial. RNAG pressure-attribution is published for Cycle 3 only; 2022 was an interim update (chemical "does not require assessment"; overall class not re-issued), so year-on-year "trend" reading is limited. The river is also a heavily-modified water body — its ecological target is "good potential", not a pristine reference state. RAW-PULL
